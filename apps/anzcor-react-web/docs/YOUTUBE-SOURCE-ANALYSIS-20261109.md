# YouTube source audit: disability simulation and cerebral palsy
**Inspected 9 November 2026. Purpose: design patterns and ethical scrutiny, not treatment recommendations.**

## 1. Supplied video — directly inspected
- Title: *What's it like to live with a disability? An introduction to the Empathy Simulation Training Suit*
- Sydney Health, published 2020-04-30, 3:26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6qOGZvKZOLQ
- **Access:** metadata, transcript and timecoded visual walkthrough inspected.
- **00:24–00:50:** classroom demonstration; orange PAUL suit and rollator.
- **00:59–01:33:** fitting webbing/straps, collars, tinted eyewear, earplugs, gloves and ankle weights.
- **01:33–01:51:** students with assistants negotiating pavements, entrances and public spaces.
- **01:52–02:57:** interviews, walking and food preparation tasks.
- Presenters advocate empathy benefits. **No evidence of improved clinical care is demonstrated by the video itself.** Restricting a non-disabled person's movement for a few hours does not reproduce life with cerebral palsy or other disability. Such exercises risk teaching temporary helplessness rather than disability-led environmental and clinical redesign.

## 3. Evidence testing the central ethical claim
- Hicks E C et al. (2022), PMID:36188992, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36188992/ — qualitative analysis found disability simulation could generate embarrassment/stigmatisation while participatory accessibility audits better centred disabled people's experience.
- Ma GYK & Mak WWS (2022), PMID:35687538, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35687538/ — meta-analysis of 12 studies reported mixed, sometimes adverse attitudinal effects; increased fear/anxiety alongside small changes in some inclusion attitudes.
- Bennett P et al. (2022), PMID:35279918, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35279918/ — Australian aged-care suit evaluation reported improvements in **self-assessed** compassion/PCC, with no established sustained practice impact.
- Levett-Jones et al. (2017), PMID:28957727, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28957727/ — nursing student point-of-view exercise reported increased immediate empathy scores; evidence does not establish authentic lived disability representation or patient outcomes.
- Dynamic, clinical, peer-reviewed and lived-experience findings must remain separate, with conflicts and limits transparent.

## 4. AURA product decision
**Adopt:** multiple learner perspectives, observed workflow barriers, role coordination, scenario briefing, supervised practice, accessible debrief, equipment readiness, and participant control.
**Redesign:** make the disabled person a knowledgeable collaborator; give participants access to realistic AAC and accurate accessibility choices; measure what clinicians actually do to remove barriers.
**Do not adopt:** empathy suits as a proxy for identity, distress scoring as empathy, cure narrative, VR wrist movement accuracy as general CP outcome, visual accuracy as clinical validation, or any unreviewed invasive/medication instruction.
